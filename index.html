How stat targets are calculated

For each player and statistic, sort the selected last 5, 10 or 20 collected values from highest to lowest. Require at least ceil(hit rate × sample size) appearances at or above a target, then select the highest value that meets that requirement. Equality counts as a hit. For example, at 90% across 10 games, the ninth-highest value is the target; ties can raise its observed hit rate above 90%.

Targets come directly from observed values, rather than a sportsbook line catalog. The full history window is required. Missing statistics and future observations are excluded; missing appearances can affect the record. A 90% historical hit rate is not a validated 90% chance for the next game. Zero or negative targets are shown when the data support no higher total.

Ranking uses the historical evidence lower bound, then a shrunk consistency estimate. These remain descriptive summaries after searching many statistics. Fresh player data and injury coverage are required; uncertain or unavailable players are excluded. Opponent effects, minutes and starters are unconfirmed. Results include the original game values for inspection. Multiple props can be correlated.

Research methods & sources

Use rolling game logs, sample sizes, role and playing-time context, and chronological testing. Streaks alone do not demonstrate a betting edge. Missing appearances are excluded, not scored as losses or zeros. Integer-line ties are shown as pushes, although platform settlement rules vary.
